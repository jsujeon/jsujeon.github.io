Abstract

Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) are increasingly used as automatic judges for evaluating generated speech, yet existing benchmarks focus predominantly on overall naturalness while neglecting fine-grained paralinguistic qualities. We introduce ParaPairAudioBench, a diagnostic pairwise benchmark containing 5,175 audio pairs spanning five paralinguistic dimensions: Style, Rate, Emphasis, Age, and Gender.

Beyond binary preference, our benchmark incorporates tie conditions to assess whether models can appropriately abstain under ambiguity, and distinguishes same-transcript from cross-transcript pairs to isolate acoustic reliance from lexical shortcuts. Experiments across commercial and open-source LALMs reveal that even the strongest model, Gemini 2.5 Flash, falls 17.7%p below human performance on average, with current models lagging by up to 32%p overall. We further uncover systematic calibration failures in tie cases and asymmetric transcript-dependency patterns, highlighting critical reliability gaps in current LALM judges.

Benchmark Design

Five Paralinguistic Dimensions

We evaluate models across two categories of paralinguistic attributes:

Static speaker characteristics: determined by vocal tract physiology:

Each criterion captures a distinct aspect of paralinguistic speech quality, enabling multi-dimensional, criterion-specific diagnosis of LALM judges.

Key Design Principles

Tie Conditions

Each instance allows three decisions. Tie cases include "Both Good" (both satisfy the criterion) and "Both Bad" (neither does), exposing whether models can abstain under ambiguity.

 Instruction: Which audio clip better expresses the "happy" style?

Audio A
"She waved goodbye." 😕 confused
Audio B
"She waved goodbye." 🤫 whisper

Neither sounds happy. Correct answer:

[[A]] [[B]] [[Tie]] ✓

Transcript Control

Pairs are split between same-transcript (47%) and cross-transcript (53%) conditions, isolating whether models genuinely process acoustics or exploit lexical shortcuts.

Key Findings

Performance Gap

Even the best model (Gemini 2.5 Flash) falls 17.7%p below humans on average; the overall gap across models reaches 32%p. Models and humans differ in which criteria are hardest: models struggle most with Style, while humans find Age most difficult (κ = 0.365). Notably, Gemini exceeds human performance on Age by 3.8%p.

Calibration Failure on Tie Cases

Models systematically force a preference instead of abstaining. Gemini drops from 82.3% → 19.3% (Non-Tie → Tie) on Emphasis; GPT-4o Audio collapses from 69.0% → 3.8% on Style. SpeechJudge almost never selects Tie (1.7% on Style). Humans maintain 75%+ Tie accuracy across criteria.

Style vs. Emphasis Reversal

For Style, models over-rely on text: Gemini drops 47.2%p from same- to cross-transcript (83.8% → 36.6%), while humans stay stable (86% → 85%). For Emphasis, the pattern reverses, cross-transcript pairs actually help models (Gemini: 43.5% → 56.4%), suggesting limited local prosodic sensitivity.

Positional Bias

All models show systematic input-order preference. SpeechJudge has the largest Acc@A − Acc@B gap at 29.4%p, always favoring position B. Qwen2.5-Omni consistently favors position A across all criteria. Model ranking by accuracy, consistency, and ConsistentAcc is identical, Gemini leads and SpeechJudge trails on all three.

Analysis

Calibration: Non-Tie vs. Tie Accuracy

Models that discriminate well in Non-Tie cases frequently collapse on Tie cases. The gap is sharpest for Emphasis and Style: Gemini drops from 82.3% to 19.3% on Emphasis, and GPT-4o Audio from 69.0% to 3.8% on Style. In contrast, humans maintain 75%+ Tie accuracy across criteria. On Age, open-source models show higher Tie than Non-Tie accuracy, but since Non-Tie is already near chance, this reflects task failure rather than calibration competence.

Transcript Influence: Acoustic vs. Lexical Reliance

Style: Models over-rely on text. Gemini drops 47.2%p from same- to cross-transcript (83.8% → 36.6%), while humans stay stable (86% → 85%).

Emphasis: The pattern reverses. Cross-transcript pairs help models (Gemini: 43.5% → 56.4%), suggesting limited local prosodic sensitivity within identical utterances. Humans again remain stable.
